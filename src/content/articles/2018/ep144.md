**Episode Summary:** A chapter from Mike's eBook *How To Podcast on Wordpress.com*, sharing 14 reasons to consider WordPress.com as a combined podcast and website host. **Note: plan names and pricing reflect 2018 and have since changed.**

## What You'll Learn

- 14 reasons WordPress.com made sense as an all-in-one podcast and website host in 2018
- The three WordPress.com plan tiers at the time and what each unlocked
- How WordPress.com handled reliability, customization, and multiple/private podcast feeds
- Monetization options available through WordPress.com hosting

Mike's approach to recommendations: he only endorses tools he genuinely uses and believes in, because trust — that he knows what he's talking about and actually believes it — is the foundation of the whole brand. These are the 14 reasons that were compelling enough to write an entire eBook about it: [How To Podcast on Wordpress.com: The Step-By-Step Guide](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/wppodcast).

## 14 Reasons to Use WordPress.com

**1. Simplicity.** One price covers both podcast and website hosting. Mike's own setup at the time wasn't simple — separate bills to Libsyn, Siteground, and Sucuri (for security, after a website hack) — which made the WordPress.com all-in-one approach appealing.

**2. Trust.** WordPress has a long track record and roughly 30% of websites run on WordPress.org or WordPress.com. Paid plans included email and live chat support.

**3. Affordability.** Every paid plan bundles podcast, blog, and website hosting plus a free custom domain. Note: the Free Plan doesn't support audio uploads, so podcasting requires at least a paid tier. At the time: the **Personal Plan** ($4/month, 6GB — roughly 200 thirty-minute episodes worth of storage), the **Premium Plan** ($8.25/month, 13GB, premium themes, video support), and the **Business Plan** ($24.92/month, unlimited storage/bandwidth, plugin installs, custom themes like Divi, automatic backups, e-commerce). See [current WordPress.com pricing](https://wordpress.com/pricing/) for today's plans and rates.

**4. Reliability.** WordPress.com's server infrastructure is built for uptime, security, backups, and heavy bandwidth — with enough redundancy that an individual site going down independent of a platform-wide outage is unlikely, unlike smaller shared-hosting setups.

**5. Customization.** The base tier is limited to free themes; the mid-tier unlocks hundreds of premium, mobile-responsive themes; the top tier allows full custom theme uploads (Divi, in Mike's case). For a podcast that just needs a home base, the entry-level plan is often enough.

**6. Domain name included.** Paid plans include a free custom domain for the first year (renewal was $13/year at the time), replacing the default `yourpodcast.wordpress.com` address — or you can bring your own existing domain.

**7. Social sharing.** One-click publishing to Twitter, Facebook, and other platforms, plus automatic email alerts to blog subscribers when a new episode goes live.

**8. Scheduling.** Prep episodes in advance and schedule them to publish automatically — useful for maintaining a regular schedule while traveling or batching production.

**9. Flexibility.** Podcast name, description, cover art, and even individual episode audio/titles/show notes can all be changed after publishing — helpful for anyone still figuring things out early on.

**10. Multiple shows.** Multiple public podcasts and RSS feeds can run from one WordPress.com site, each submitted independently to directories.

**11. Private podcasts.** Private RSS feeds don't appear in directory search but can be shared and added manually — useful for internal business communications, school courses, or selling access to bonus/archive content.

**12. Monetization.** PayPal integration supports e-commerce (podcast swag, private feed access), Patreon-style recurring monthly payments, selling older episodes via a private feed, selling bonus content, and WordPress.com's own affiliate program for referring other podcasters to the platform.

**13. Mobile app.** A free WordPress management app (iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, Linux) — not for listeners, but for managing the site and podcast on the go: stats, comment moderation, posting.

**14. Own your content.** Content created on WordPress.com is exportable at any time — a meaningful advantage of self-hosting over some fully closed third-party platforms.

## Quick Recap

Simplicity (one bill), trust (an established name), affordability ($4/month at the entry tier), reliability, customization, a free included domain, easy social sharing, scheduling, flexibility to edit after publishing, multiple shows, private feeds, monetization options, a mobile app, and full ownership of exported content.
