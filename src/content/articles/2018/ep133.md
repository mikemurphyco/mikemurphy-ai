**Episode Summary:** A start-to-finish walkthrough of Mike's 2018 backup and digital organization system — the tools, the hardware, and the redundancy strategy behind protecting years of podcast episodes, videos, photos, and business documents.

## What You'll Learn

- 3 practical tips to take inventory and get control of scattered digital data
- The exact 2018 hardware and software backup system Mike used, start to finish
- Why redundancy (a cloned drive plus offsite cloud backup) matters more than any single backup
- 4 pieces of parting advice for building a backup system that actually protects you

This episode piggybacks on [Episode 132: The iCloud Show](/podcast/ep132/).

**A question worth sitting with:** if a backpack with your laptop, iPhone, iPad, and an external hard drive got stolen out of a parked car right now, could you recover your podcast episodes, videos, photos, music library, and business documents?

## Practical Tip #1: Take Inventory

Figure out where all your digital content actually lives, and gather it in one place — consolidating from internal drives, thumb drives, external drives, Google Drive, and Dropbox. Create one folder (a "2018 Backup" folder, for example) and organize by big-ticket categories: Videos, Photos, Documents. The goal is a single master folder to back up.

## Practical Tip #2: Clean House

While consolidating, delete anything no longer needed — unused applications, desktop and Downloads folder clutter, loose files in photo/movie/music folders. Store documents in Documents, not on the Desktop. Toss any empty external drives that are several years old.

## Practical Tip #3: iOS Devices

Clean up iOS devices and export all photos and videos to consolidate with the master photo library on a desktop or laptop.

## Tools Used

**ChronoSync** ($50) — a Mac utility that scans two drives or folders and reports the differences, so you can copy over exactly what's changed and keep a backup as an exact clone, saving significant time.

**SuperDuper** ($30) — similar to ChronoSync, with scheduled automatic backups between two external drives, keeping a cloned backup running continuously without manual tracking. **Mike's preference: SuperDuper.**

## The 2018 Backup System

**iPhone and iPad** — automatically backed up to iCloud (contacts, notes, messages, photos). Photos and videos also get exported via Image Capture to a Mac and imported into Adobe Lightroom for management.

**MacBook Pro** — backed up continuously via Apple Time Machine to a drive connected to an Apple AirPort Extreme, physically separate from the computer. If the laptop failed, everything could be restored from Time Machine almost immediately.

**External drives** — LaCie Thunderbolt drives, bought in pairs: one for active work, one as a clone kept in sync via SuperDuper or ChronoSync. Fast, quiet, and reliable, though genuinely fast multi-TB SSDs were still pricey at the time, making Thunderbolt/USB 3.0 the practical choice.

**On the desk:** a 6TB LaCie drive plugged into the laptop, daisy-chained to an identical 6TB clone that never leaves the desk, plus a portable 4TB LaCie Rugged drive for client work and travel.

**Backblaze** ($5/month) — a cloud backup service continuously backing up the main 6TB drive and the MacBook Pro (on top of Time Machine and iCloud), similar to Carbonite. Nearly 3 years of reliable, set-it-and-forget-it use at the time, with occasional logins just to confirm everything's still working.

## Recap

MacBook Pro → backed up via Time Machine over WiFi. A 6TB LaCie drive holds all working media — the digital hub — mirrored by an identical clone drive for redundancy. Backblaze continuously backs up one of the 6TB drives offsite, so a fire, theft, flood, or hurricane doesn't mean losing everything.

## The Final Piece: Google Drive

The last layer serves productivity and backup at once: upgrading a G Suite account to the $10/month business plan, unlocking 1TB of Google Drive space for personal and business documents, photos, and music libraries — everything except video, which stays on the local drive system due to file size. Cloud-based and accessible from an iPhone anywhere, this update rounds out the whole business's storage needs for $10/month.

## Parting Advice

1. If you're already backup-savvy and diligent, well done. If not, at minimum use Apple Time Machine on a Mac — it has saved countless accidentally-trashed files.
2. iCloud Storage at $9.99/month for 2TB <!-- REVIEW: iCloud pricing has changed since 2018 — verify current rates --> can back up an entire computer, accessible from a phone app or browser.
3. For G Suite users, the Google Drive storage upgrade (roughly $5 extra/month at the time) removes dependence on external hard drives for anything but video, and Google's search makes finding files fast from any device.
4. Invest in good external hard drives if you're a content creator — fast read/write speeds and a solid connector type (SSD, Thunderbolt, USB 3.0) matter a lot, especially for video editing.

## Moral of the Story

Back up your digital world — it matters, for both personal peace of mind and business continuity.
