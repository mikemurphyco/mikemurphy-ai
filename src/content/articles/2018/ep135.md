**Episode Summary:** A deep dive into Amazon Alexa — how the voice assistant works, what it can do, how Amazon Skills and Flash Briefings function, and how to get a podcast onto Alexa devices via TuneIn.

## What You'll Learn

- What Amazon Alexa is, how it works, and what the Echo and Echo Dot devices do
- The range of things Alexa could do in 2018: Q&A, streaming, smart home control, shopping
- What Amazon Skills and Flash Briefings are, and how to create one for your podcast
- How to get a podcast listed on Alexa devices via TuneIn

**Pro tip for podcasters and livestreamers:** mute the microphone on top of an Echo or Dot before recording — the button turns the ring red and stops Alexa from responding, which matters a lot when recording a podcast episode that keeps saying her name.

## What Is Amazon Alexa, and How Does It Work?

Alexa is a cloud-based, voice-activated virtual assistant Amazon launched in 2014. The two most popular devices at the time were the Echo ($99, tall cylinder shape) and the Echo Dot ($50, hockey-puck shaped). <!-- REVIEW: original included mikemurphy.co/echo and mikemurphy.co/dot affiliate links to buy these on Amazon — dropped since 2018 device models/pricing have since changed --> Alexa uses AI to listen, understand, and answer most questions, with capabilities always updating automatically. You wake her by saying her name, then speak your request — she's always on and always listening. Settings are managed through the iOS or Android app.

**Origin of the name:** "Alexa" was chosen partly because the hard "X" consonant is easier to recognize accurately, and partly as a nod to the ancient Library of Alexandria — reportedly also inspired by Star Trek's computer voice system. If "Alexa" happens to be your name or a household member's, the wake word can be changed.

## What Can Alexa Do?

- **Questions and answers** — much like Siri or Google.
- **Streaming** — radio, Amazon Prime Music, Spotify, podcasts, even bedtime stories.
- **Productivity and tasks** — alarms, timers, to-do lists, calendar checks, kitchen measurement conversions.
- **Information and news** — weather, news, sports scores.
- **Calling** — voice or message other Alexa device owners.
- **Smart home control** — lights, thermostats, and other compatible devices.
- **Fun and games** — jokes, songs, puns, trivia-style games like Jeopardy.
- **Shopping** — order directly from Amazon (and, in some markets, Domino's, Uber, or Starbucks), with parental/household restrictions available.

## Amazon Skills and Flash Briefings

Amazon Skills expand what Alexa devices can do — think of them like iPhone apps, with thousands already available from third-party developers, enabled through the Alexa app or Amazon's website.

**Flash Briefings** are a special type of Skill delivering short news and information updates from sources including blog and podcast RSS feeds. Once enabled, a listener can simply ask Alexa for their flash briefing, or bundle it into a "Routine" alongside their to-do list, calendar, and weather.

### How to Make a Flash Briefing

This isn't a full tutorial, just a starting point. Podcasters already have the easiest path — a Flash Briefing can be built directly from an existing podcast RSS feed. Sign up for an [Amazon Developer account](https://developer.amazon.com/); Amazon provides tutorials to walk through the process. The RSS feed must be valid and served over secure HTTPS.

### RSS Feed Issues and How to Fix Them

Mike's own RSS feed was rejected on the first attempt — here's what happened and how it got resolved:

1. **Your RSS feed must be secure (HTTPS)**, or Amazon rejects it outright.
2. **Let's Encrypt SSL certificates didn't work** for Flash Briefings at the time — despite being a free, popular way to add HTTPS to a WordPress site, Let's Encrypt wasn't on Amazon's accepted certificate list.

**Workaround for Powerpress users on Let's Encrypt:** create a mirror of the RSS feed using Blubrry's [PodcastMirror.com](http://podcastmirror.com/) service. <!-- REVIEW: PodcastMirror.com — verify this Blubrry service still exists before treating as current guidance -->

**Mike's actual fix:** since his media files were hosted on Libsyn, he used the Libsyn RSS feed instead — which didn't have the "s" at the end of `http://` by default — and simply added the "s" manually to make it `https://` before submitting it to the Flash Briefing application. That resolved the rejection and the Flash Briefing went live.

## How Do You Listen to Podcasts on Alexa?

Simply say "Alexa, play Mike Murphy Unplugged on TuneIn" and Alexa pulls up the latest episode. You can rewind, fast-forward, and pause, or browse and play from the Alexa phone app.

**What is TuneIn?** [TuneIn](https://tunein.com/) was the default podcast directory on Alexa at the time — a free internet radio and podcast directory, similar to iTunes, Stitcher, or Spreaker. Spotify and iHeartRadio also served up podcasts on Alexa.

### How to Get Your Podcast Into the TuneIn Directory

Search TuneIn first — many shows are already listed automatically. If not, [submit your RSS feed through TuneIn's broadcaster form](http://tunein.com/syndication/new/?IsBroadcaster=true). A real person processes the request, so allow a day or two before following up.
