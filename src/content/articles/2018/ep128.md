**Episode Summary:** A recap of Podfest Multimedia Expo 2018 — why Mike keeps going back year after year, and the four biggest lessons he brought home this time.

## What You'll Learn

- What Podfest Multimedia Expo is and how Mike's reasons for attending evolved over 3 years
- Why in-person industry events matter, even for self-described introverts
- 4 key takeaways from Podfest 2018: collaboration, feedback, authenticity, and not undervaluing yourself
- The book Principles by Ray Dalio and its lesson on the value of honest feedback

## What Is Podfest Multimedia Expo?

An educational and training conference for independent podcasters, created by Chris and Katie Krimitsos, originally based in Tampa before moving to Orlando to draw a more international audience. Podfest 2018 ran February 8-10 at the Wyndham Orlando Resort, with early bird pricing at $249 and hotel rates around $139/night. The schedule is dense and tightly run, with both shared main sessions and breakout tracks — 2018 introduced a dedicated YouTube track for video creators.

## Why Mike Went

2018 marked his third year attending. **Year one (2016):** a new podcaster looking for general advice and trying to get his name in front of the community. **Year two (2017):** more focused on Mike Murphy LLC specifically, absorbing as much as possible from people already succeeding and looking for chances to help others. **Year three (2018):** almost entirely about networking — building stronger relationships in a space he knew he'd be part of for the long haul.

Podfest keeps a spot on the calendar every year because of its unusually kind, generous community — podcasters, creators, and entrepreneurs genuinely trying to help each other, largely a reflection of the organizers themselves.

Even as a self-described introvert who finds conference small talk draining, Mike pushes through — no excuses. Being around like-minded creative entrepreneurs, all working hard and learning from each other, makes attending worth the discomfort every time.

## Takeaways From Podfest 2018

Four things stood out, beyond a bad cold:

1. **Collaboration is key.** Research people worth connecting with and find genuine ways to help them — even something as simple as a helpful comment or Instagram DM builds a real working relationship.
2. **Honest feedback needs to be a priority.** Making it easier for the audience to leave feedback (in show notes visible on mobile, and more direct calls to action) came directly out of this realization. **Book mentioned:** [Principles by Ray Dalio](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/principles) — Dalio credits radically honest feedback as a major reason for his own success and his $150 billion company's.
3. **Being authentic is powerful.** A presenter fumbling with a broken PowerPoint tossed his notes aside and asked the room if he could just ditch the slides and be himself — the room visibly relaxed, and it became one of the most memorable moments of the conference. The lesson: people want you, not the packaging.
4. **Undervaluing your own skills isn't an option.** A recurring struggle — confidence in the work itself, but reluctance to actually charge what it's worth, often overdelivering instead. Conversations at Podfest 2018 reframed this: what feels obvious to you is often genuinely valuable and impressive to someone else.

## Conclusion

Podfest is an annual event in Orlando, Florida, and a strong recommendation for both new and experienced podcasters, along with anyone building an online business around video or other new media. It keeps growing, but retains an intimate, mutually-supportive community feel that's hard to find elsewhere — solving both technical problems and creating friendships, clients, and opportunities that don't happen the same way over social media alone.
