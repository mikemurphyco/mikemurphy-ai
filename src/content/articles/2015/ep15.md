**Episode Summary:** Part 2 of the Learn, Create, Move Forward trilogy — what content creation actually means (it's not the same as creativity), and the full list of things created regularly to serve one specific audience.

## What You'll Learn

- The real definition of content creation, and why it's not the same as creativity
- A full list of the content types created regularly: website, blog, video, podcast, and more
- Why your only real focus should be creating for your specific audience, or "tribe"
- Whether a professional website needs a custom developer or a template will do

[Part 1](/podcast/ep14/) covered where to learn. This episode covers creation, not to be confused with creativity. [Part 3](/podcast/ep16/) covers moving forward.

## Ask Mike Anything: Template or Custom Developer?

**Question:** Does a professional-looking website for a mortgage business need a hired web developer/designer, or will a template work?

**Answer:** WordPress templates and frameworks work for most scenarios today — coding a website from scratch isn't necessary to get a professional site up quickly. **Template resources:** [ThemeForest](http://themeforest.com), [Elegant Themes](http://elegantthemes.com), [Squarespace](http://squarespace.com), [WooThemes](http://woothemes.com).

## 1. What Is Content Creation?

Per Wikipedia: content is "something that is to be expressed through some medium, as speech, writing or any of various arts" for self-expression, distribution, marketing, and/or publication. Typical forms include maintaining and updating websites, blogging, photography, videography, podcasting, managing social media accounts, and editing/distributing digital media like audio, video, or design.

The only real focus needs to be creating content for **your** audience — or, as Seth Godin calls it, your tribe. What does that specific audience need? Don't let "infobesity" — the feeling that there's already too much content out there — become a reason not to bother. Your voice is unique, and your audience needs you specifically to create content that helps them.

**Note:** Sean McCabe has a great video addressing this directly, called ["Who Am I to Try"](http://seanwes.com/tv/114-too-many-people-already-do-what-i-want-to-do/).

## 2. What Gets Created, and How

Website, blog posts, videos, classes, podcast episodes, artwork, lead magnets, email newsletters, tutorials, behind-the-scenes videos.

**Classes taught on Skillshare:** [Photoshop: The Art of the Headswap](https://www.skillshare.com/classes/photography/Photoshop-The-Art-of-the-Head-Swap/1700917171) and [WordPress Cheatsheet for Entrepreneurs](https://www.skillshare.com/classes/technology/Wordpress-For-Entrepreneurs-The-Quick-Easy-Install-Cheatsheet/1105132078).

The big-ticket items already covered — website, blog, videos, classes, podcast — are the core. Everything else fills in the gaps: artwork and photos for the brand and blog, lead magnets to grow an email list (like the Unplugged Guide of Resources and the Microphone Guide), the email newsletter itself as a communication channel, tutorials, behind-the-scenes videos, and more.
