**Episode Summary:** Why anyone would listen to yet another podcast on a crowded topic — it comes down to perspective — plus 5 real benefits of teaching and 5 concrete ways to start teaching online.

## What You'll Learn

- 5 real benefits of teaching: authority, skill-building, service, marketing, and revenue
- 5 ways to teach online: blog, YouTube, courses, social media, or a podcast
- Why perspective — not the topic itself — is what makes a podcast unique
- Why niching down is hard for a jack-of-all-trades, and why teaching helps anyway

## Ask Mike Anything: Why Would Anyone Listen to Me?

**Question (from the Blab chatroom):** I want to start a tech podcast, but there are already so many. Why would anyone listen to me?

**Answer:** Perspective — credit to teacher and podcaster [Chris Nesi](http://chrisnesi.com) for the framing. This show is unique because of the perspective brought to every topic, not the topic itself. Watch the evening news on any given night — the same story sounds completely different depending on the station or reporter covering it. The reason someone listens, even to a familiar topic, comes down to the relationship and connection built with the audience. That perspective will resonate with some listeners and not others, and developing a real voice and style takes time, practice, and experience. But genuine passion and knowledge, without worrying about the competition, is what actually gets people listening.

## 5 Benefits of Teaching

1. **Authority.** The more a topic gets taught, the more people associate the teacher with it as an expert. Authority in any industry opens doors and builds confidence. Niching down is genuinely hard for a jack-of-all-trades — this has been an ongoing struggle with too many interests to easily pick just one — but teaching consistently is still the answer.
2. **Learning & improving skills.** Teaching is the best way to learn and master a skill. Writing podcast scripts and explaining topics to others has taught more than almost anything else — every lesson forces the questions: what's the purpose here, what steps are required, what should people take away? Deconstructing a process to explain it simply is a skill in itself.
3. **Helping others.** Genuinely rewarding — watching someone go from frustrated or overwhelmed to unstuck is deeply satisfying. Helping others, through social media "serving hours" or otherwise, sharpens skills while building real relationships and a strong network. Word of mouth follows naturally.
4. **Marketing & promotion.** Teaching is the ultimate online marketing tool — sharing knowledge gets a brand in front of people in a positive light. Sharing a great article instead of a breakfast photo, or guest-writing for someone respected, provides real value and reaches new audiences. The more people associate a brand with genuine, useful content, the more that brand grows.
5. **Financial.** Teaching can genuinely generate serious income — through consulting, coaching, or training courses.

## 5 Ways to Teach Online

1. **Start a website & blog.** Still one of the easiest ways to start teaching and sharing knowledge — infographics, "Top 10" list posts, embedded video with bullet-point breakdowns, all work. A blog is also where an email list grows, so the more genuinely useful material published, the more people sign up to stay informed.
2. **Start a YouTube channel.**
3. **Create online courses.** Resources mentioned: [Udemy](http://udemy.com), [Skillshare](http://skillshare.com), [Teachery](http://teachery.co), [Thinkific](http://thinkific.com).
4. **Social media.** Build relationships and share knowledge directly. Resources mentioned: [Blab](http://blab.im), [Periscope](http://periscope.tv).
5. **Launch a podcast.** One of the most effective teaching platforms online, and a great way to establish authority and mastery of a topic. [Omar Zenhom of $100 MBA](http://100mba.net/category/show/feed/) teaches 10-minute business lessons 7 days a week and does it very effectively. [Sean McCabe](http://seanwes.com/feed/podcast) teaches entrepreneurship the same way. Both use podcasting to add value, build relationships, and market products they sell.
