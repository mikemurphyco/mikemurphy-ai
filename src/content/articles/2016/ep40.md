**Episode Summary:** Why write a full script before recording? The 5 real benefits of scripting a podcast episode, plus the exact tools used to write and format every one.

## What You'll Learn

- Why submitting an RSS feed once to each directory is all a podcast ever needs
- 5 real benefits of writing a full script before recording
- How scripting turns into blog posts, tutorials, and eventually a book
- The two tools used to write scripts and format show notes

## Ask Mike Anything: Will My Podcast Auto-Distribute to Every Directory?

**Question:** When I launch my podcast and submit it to iTunes, will my podcast automatically be distributed to all of the other podcast directories?

**Answer:** No. The RSS feed has to be submitted manually to each directory a show wants to appear in — but only once. **Popular directories:** [iTunes](https://itunesconnect.apple.com/itc/static/login?view=5&path=%2FWebObjects%2FiTunesConnect.woa%3F), [Stitcher](https://www.stitcher.com/), [Spreaker](http://www.spreaker.com/), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/music/podcasts/publish?u=0). **Resource:** [Podcast Places](https://podcastplaces.com) lists submission links for every major directory. **Bottom line:** submit the RSS feed once per directory, and every new episode published after that updates each directory automatically.

## Benefits of Writing a Podcast Script

1. **Preparation.** Writing a script forces real homework and research up front — by the time it's done, the material is known inside and out, which makes recording day far less stressful.
2. **Recording time.** Working off a script (and rehearsing while writing it) makes recording dramatically more efficient — fewer "umms" and "ahhs," less re-taking.
3. **Knowledge & authority.** The act of writing forces a deeper understanding of the topic than talking through it off the cuff ever would.
4. **Writing skill.** Podcasting has meant a lot of practice at a completely new, more technical and organized style of writing than college English ever demanded.
5. **Repurposing power.** A finished script becomes the raw material for blog posts, Medium articles, tutorials, vlogs — and eventually, a book.
