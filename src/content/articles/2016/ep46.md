## What Is Screencasting?

Simply recording the screen of a device — laptop, iPhone, or iPad — using dedicated software (a camera pointed at a monitor is the low-tech alternative). It's often called "over the shoulder" video, since it shares the screen exactly as the presenter sees it — a genuinely effective teaching approach.

**Ways to use screencasting:** training employees, briefing virtual assistants, explaining or teaching anything done on a computer or iDevice, and communicating with clients about a project (designers, especially).

## Recipe for Good Screencasts

**Tip 1: Preparation.** Write an outline or detailed notes before pressing record — figure out exactly what the video needs to cover ahead of time.

**Mike's screencast recipe:**

1. Who is this video for?
2. What is the purpose of this video?
3. What should the viewer learn or take away from it?
4. What steps will be covered, and in what order?
5. What's the simplest way to summarize the lesson, step by step?
6. What should the viewer do after finishing the video? Include a call to action.

**Tip 2:** cover everything promised in the title and description.

**Tip 3:** be concise and to the point.

## Advantages of Simple Screencast Videos

- Minimal, inexpensive gear required — a computer and microphone will do it
- A camera isn't necessary
- Good lighting isn't required
- Content ideas never run out — anything done on a computer or iDevice can become a screencast
- Screencasting sharpens teaching and communication skills
- Video editing skills improve overall
- Sharing more builds real expertise over time
- Screencasting processes surfaces both strengths and weaknesses
- Screencasts spread easily across every social channel
- Audience engagement improves — helpful screencasts get a response
- Screencasts are the building blocks for classes, tutorials, and online education generally
