## Podcast Statistics

50% of all new podcasters quit before episode 7. Of the 50% who make it to episode 7, only half make it to episode 15. Podcasters who reach episode 25 have a really high chance of lasting 2 years or more. Making it to episode 25 doesn't sound like a huge number, but according to the stats, it's genuinely a big deal.

## I. What Is Podfade?

**Podfade (per Urban Dictionary):** when a podcast starts putting out episodes more sporadically and at greater intervals — usually starting with one missed episode, but compounding over time, sometimes as severe as one episode every other month. Podfade often leads to podcast death.

In simpler terms: podfade is when podcasters lose interest or get too busy, and stop producing shows on schedule until they eventually stop altogether.

## II. Why Does Podfade Happen?

**Common causes:**

- **Lack of time.** People underestimate the time investment required, life takes over, and the schedule slips until the show ultimately stops.
- **Technical difficulty.** Recording and post-production are genuinely hard for many, and non-technical folks often just move on.
- **Skills.** Some people hate the sound of their own voice or feel uncomfortable on mic, and aren't willing to put in the time to get past it.
- **Topics.** Running out of things to talk about happens to a lot of podcasters.
- **Costs.** Spending money on a hobby isn't always practical.
- **No income.** Some enter podcasting expecting sponsors and affiliate income quickly, and podfade when it doesn't materialize.
- **Crickets.** Podcasting can feel lonely early on with a small audience — pressing record and getting little or no feedback leads a lot of people to think, "why bother, nobody cares what I have to say."
- **Boredom.** Some people just don't enjoy it and want to do something else — if it's not fun, motivation eventually runs out.
- **Guests.** Interview shows can struggle to find quality guests, or find the whole booking process a hassle.
- **Co-hosts.** Some shows start with a friend, and when the friend quits, the show goes with them.

## III. How to Prevent Podfade

1. **Be realistic — a pre-launch step.** Be practical, plan for the time required, and don't bite off more than can be chewed.
2. **Simplify.** Look at the entire process start to finish and cut anything unnecessary — script writing/research, show notes, editing, marketing. **Bottom line:** simplifying means making the whole process efficient and repeatable. Never stop fine-tuning each step, and eventually the whole thing runs on autopilot. Think of the podcast workflow as one template, and fix whatever part keeps causing friction.
3. **Network.** Blab, and social media channels like Facebook, Twitter, and Google+.
4. **Learn.** Read, watch tutorials, ask experts, go to events — like [Podfest](http://podfest.us) or [Podcast Movement](http://podcastmovement.com).
5. **Look in the mirror (gut-check).** Is the direction things are moving actually the direction you want to go?

## In Conclusion

Podfade happens for a variety of reasons. If podcasting is loved but too difficult, simplify and figure out exactly what's not working, then fix that specific part. Maybe a breather is needed — breaking a show into "seasons" is a trending way to do that. Maybe solo shows feel boring or lonely — switching to interviews or bringing in a co-host can fix that. Nothing worth doing is easy at first, but if podcasting matters for your business or well-being, it's worth fighting for and fixing before it fades away.
