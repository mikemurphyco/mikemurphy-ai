**Episode Summary:** The art of making tutorials — how to find topics worth teaching, a real 3-step production process, and a genuine outreach script for landing influencers and high-profile guests.

## What You'll Learn

- A real script for reaching out to influencers and high-profile guest candidates
- Where to find genuinely good tutorial topic ideas
- The 3-step process: script, record, edit
- Why making tutorials is worth the time, beyond just the content itself

## Ask Mike Anything: Landing Influencers and High-Profile Guests

**Question:** How do you get influencers and high-profile guests on your show?

**Answer:** Build a real relationship and connection with anyone worth having as a guest first. Start small, stay respectful — this is planting a seed, not screaming for attention or stalking someone's feed. Comment thoughtfully on a blog post or Medium article, then another, adding genuine value. Retweet a few of their tweets. Treat the potential guest like any other person worth building a relationship with. Then send an email along these lines: "Hey [Name], I'm finding a lot of value in your content. I particularly liked your article on X, and your podcast episode about Y was spot on. I have a weekly podcast where I interview leading entrepreneurs like yourself, and I'd love to introduce you to my listeners and promote your new book. Would you be available for a 20-minute interview sometime in the next 2 months?"

## What Is a Tutorial?

Simply an educational or informational lesson about a topic — think of it like a mini lesson plan from school. Bundles of tutorials can make up a full course or class. They can be audio, video, or text, and the goal is always to teach.

## How to Find Topics to Teach

Look for topics that help people save time or do something better or faster. This can span a wide range, but only topics genuinely known well and relevant to content creation and podcasting make the cut. **Places to find topics:** questions people ask in emails, questions from listeners or online, the everyday process of running the business (share it), the applications folder on a Mac or PC (any software worth explaining?), the apps on an iPhone (any useful tips?), and inspiration from Lynda.com, Skillshare.com, and Udemy.com.

**Resource tip:** [Trello](http://trello.com) for organizing tutorial ideas and workflow.

## How to Make Tutorials

**Step 1: write a script or outline. Always.** Start with the end goal in mind — "In this tutorial, I'm going to show you how to…" What benefit will the viewer get from watching? Recap and summarize. End with a call to action (visit the website, etc.). **Pre-production tip:** organize project folders ahead of time. **Resource tip:** [Post-Haste](https://www.digitalrebellion.com/posthaste/).

**Step 2: recording & production.**

1. Live video — teaching directly in front of a camera
2. Screencasting — using something like [Screenflow](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/screenflow) or [Camtasia](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/camtasia) to record a computer or iDevice screen
3. A combination of both — mixing talking-head camera with screencasting, the typical approach here

**Step 3: editing.** Clean it up, add a logo, titles, and some music — but keep it simple. **Resource tip:** [Wistia](https://wistia.com/library).

## Where to Put Tutorials

Start with [YouTube](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/youtube), make a blog post embedding the YouTube link, and use [Buffer](http://bufferapp.com) to share on social media. **Big tip:** give the tutorial a genuinely good title.

## Why Make Tutorials?

- Helps people
- Lets people connect with you
- Establishes authority
- Drives traffic to your site and YouTube channel
- Improves knowledge, teaching style, and confidence
- Sharpens podcast script writing and overall delivery quality
- A great networking and marketing tool
