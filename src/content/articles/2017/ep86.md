## What Is a Vlog?

A blog is a regularly updated site or page, often informal and written by an individual or small group. A vlog is the video equivalent — regularly posted, conversational stories told through video instead of text.

Mark Zuckerberg predicted Facebook would be over 90% video content within 5 years — a strong signal for where content was heading. Vlogs are a genuine marketing tool in the broader content toolkit, alongside the other two pillars covered in [Episode 81: The Content Whiteboard Show](/podcast/ep81/) — audio (podcasting) and writing (blog, Medium, LinkedIn Pulse).

## Vlogs, Defined

A **vlogger** creates vlogs; **vlogging** is the act of making one. General characteristics: personal and documentary in nature, current and dynamic, built around a behind-the-scenes/day-in-the-life storytelling style, uploaded on a frequent and consistent schedule, and built around a community that follows the journey. It's genuinely hard work and requires real skill — not for everyone.

**Why watch vlogs at all?** Entertainment, creative inspiration, and learning — and only from vloggers whose skill and style earn real interest.

[Casey Neistat](https://www.youtube.com/user/caseyneistat), [Gary Vaynerchuk](https://www.youtube.com/user/GaryVaynerchuk), [Mr. Ben Brown](https://www.youtube.com/user/benbrown100), [Fun For Louis](https://www.youtube.com/user/FunForLouis), and [Sara Dietschy](https://www.youtube.com/user/saradietschy) — not a personal style match, but a genuine respect for her Adobe and video skills and hustle.

## Benefits of Vlogging

Brand exposure, repurposable content, community and audience building, stronger relationships and networks, client attraction, sales and lead generation. People are often more curious about *how* something works than the finished product itself — vlogging leans directly into that. It can also open up brand sponsorships, and it builds self-awareness, confidence, creativity, and authority over time — all essential ingredients for a strong personal brand.

## How to Start a Vlog

Start with a rough plan and a clear sense of what the content actually is — behind-the-scenes of a business or operation is a solid starting point. 5–10 minutes is a reasonable episode length.

**Ask first:** why do you want a vlog? More clients, more brand exposure, more podcast listeners, more newsletter signups? Does it sound genuinely fun and creative? **Technical basics:** a YouTube channel and video editing software.

**Gear:**

- **Camera:** an iPhone is genuinely good enough.
- **Microphone:** [Rode Smartlav+ for iOS](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/smartlav)
- **Tripod:** [Manfrotto Pixi Mini](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/pixi)
- **Lights:** [Manfrotto Lumimuse portable LED](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/lumimuse)
- [Vlog starter kit on Amazon](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/vlogstarterkit)

**Video editors (Mac):** [Adobe Premiere Pro](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/adobe) (favorite), [Camtasia](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/camtasia)/[ScreenFlow](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/screenflow) work fine too, along with iMovie or the YouTube video editor.

**The cons:** genuinely hard work, a degree of vulnerability, and video/editing is time-consuming.

**Current status:** [Instagram Stories](http://instagram.com/mikeunplugged) currently fills the vlog role and builds community in the meantime — a more permanent home on YouTube is the plan once time allows, with vlog-style videos on the way soon.
