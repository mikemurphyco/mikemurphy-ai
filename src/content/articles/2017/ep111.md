## What Is G Suite?

An all-in-one office and productivity suite from Google, built for small and large businesses, educational institutions, nonprofits, and anyone with a website. The headline feature: the power of Gmail running on your own domain, paired with an entire suite of office and communication tools comparable to Word, PowerPoint, and Excel — all branded to your business.

**Cost:** free to set up, then $5/user/month (or $60/year). A [14-day free trial with 20% off](https://go.mikemurphy.ai/gsuite) is available at signup.

## How Does G Suite Work?

It's a bundle of services accessible from any web browser. Setting it up means changing your domain's DNS settings — specifically the MX records — to point your domain's mail routing to Google. Once that's done, you can send and receive mail as `you@yourdomain.com`.

## Email Options When Starting a Business

- **Personal email (not ideal)** — Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, Hotmail, AOL. Something like `mikemurphyllc@gmail.com` works technically, but it doesn't read as professional.
- **Hosting-provided email (better, viable for some)** — most hosts (GoDaddy, Bluehost, HostGator) offer email through their cPanel. An improvement over personal email, but still less reliable than Gmail.
- **G Suite (the best by far)** — the full power of Gmail, running on your own domain.

Email is still the backbone of most businesses — it's worth using the most reliable, trustworthy option available rather than treating it as an afterthought.

## G Suite Features

- **Gmail** — customized to your business, with unlimited aliases (the part before the @, e.g. `sales@`, `info@`, `support@`, all routing wherever you want).
- **Unlimited domains** — add as many domains as needed under one account, useful for testing a new product or business idea without a second subscription.
- **24/7 support** from Google.
- **Storage** — 30GB free per user via Google Drive (Dropbox-equivalent), upgradeable to 1TB for $10/month, or unlimited for multi-user accounts.
- **Office & productivity** — full replacements for Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, accessible from any browser, with easy sharing and real-time collaboration and no version-conflict headaches.
- **Security & administration** — centralized control over billing, permissions, and user accounts; add or remove employees, reset passwords, and secure organization files from one place — essentially a lightweight IT department.
- **App Store** — mostly free add-ons that integrate with accounting software and CRMs you may already use.

**What's included:**

1. **Gmail** — second to none.
2. **Calendar** — shared and personal, essential for any business.
3. **Drive** — Dropbox-equivalent, for backup and large file sharing.
4. **Docs** — Word equivalent.
5. **Sheets** — Excel equivalent.
6. **Slides** — PowerPoint equivalent.
7. **Forms** — great for surveys.
8. **Google Hangouts** — Skype-on-steroids.
9. **Google+** — largely dead as a social network by this point, but still relevant for local discovery.

Google products tend to look minimal at first glance but pack a real punch once you dig in — it takes new users a little time to adjust, but the reliability and ease of use are hard to beat.
