**Episode Summary:** Part 2 of the self-publishing series — the 3 core Amazon tools for getting a book published, on-demand printed, and turned into an audiobook.

## What You'll Learn

- The 3 core Amazon self-publishing tools: KDP, CreateSpace, and ACX
- How Kindle Direct Publishing works and what Amazon takes in commission
- Amazon's non-Amazon self-publishing competitors worth knowing about
- What audiobook narrators actually get paid on ACX

[Part 1](/podcast/ep72/) covered writing and editing tools and resources. This episode covers self-publishing directly on Amazon.com: Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP), CreateSpace (on-demand printing), and ACX (Audiobook Creation Exchange).

## Competitors to Amazon in Self-Publishing

[Apple iBooks Store](https://www.apple.com/ibooks/), [Kobo Writing Life](https://writinglife.kobobooks.com/), [Barnes & Noble](https://www.barnesandnoble.com/), [Blurb](https://www.blurb.com/), and [Google Play Books](https://play.google.com/books), among others.

## Amazon's 3 Self-Publishing Components

### Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP)

The e-book publishing side of Amazon, launched in 2007, and how a book ends up in the Kindle Store. Completely free to use, though Amazon takes a 30% commission on sales. The Kindle Store grew from 500,000 titles in 2010 to over 5 million by this episode.

**Step 1:** create an account and profile on KDP using an Amazon login — worth doing as soon as the decision to write a book is made, just to start exploring the process. Add tax info, bank account details, the book title, and verify rights.

### CreateSpace

Also owned and operated by Amazon — on-demand printing for physical copies of a book, for a fee plus roughly a 40% commission.

### ACX

Amazon's Audiobook Creation Exchange, for turning a digital book into an audiobook. Audiobooks sell through Amazon, iTunes, and Audible (also Amazon-owned).

**How narrators get paid on ACX — 3 options:**

- Hourly rate — roughly $225/finished hour
- Royalty split — 50% of all royalties
- A combination of hourly plus royalty

One finished hour of audio equals roughly 9,300 words, and takes the average narrator about 6.2 hours to produce.
