**Episode Summary:** A storytelling episode built around a whitewater rafting trip through Cataract Canyon, Utah — using the experience to unpack self-doubt, where it really comes from, and how to use it as fuel instead of a reason to quit.

## What You'll Learn

- A whitewater rafting story from Durango, Colorado, used as a metaphor for self-doubt
- Why self-doubt from inexperience is natural — and how to use it as fuel instead of a stop sign
- The difference between your own self-doubt and absorbing other people's doubt
- Why there's no universal timeline for confidence, skill, or business success

**Dictionary definition of self-doubt:** the lack of confidence in oneself and one's abilities.

## The Whitewater Rafting Story

Years spent living in Durango, Colorado, working as a bartender, with whitewater rafting as one of the favorite activities of that chapter of life (college: University of Massachusetts–Amherst). The river at the center of this story: Cataract Canyon, Utah.

## The Moral of the Story

Self-doubt from inexperience is natural — it takes time to get great at anything, and that inexperience can be used as fuel rather than a reason to stop. A lot of what feels like self-doubt isn't even your own; it's the doubt of people around you, absorbed as if it were yours.

The fix is redirecting effort: stop spending energy proving your worth and abilities to others, and put all of it into getting from one side of the river to the other — the thing you actually set out to do, and know deep down you're capable of with a little effort. Use the self-doubt that comes from inexperience and lack of time to your advantage — let it make you hungrier and push you to work harder. What it should never be mistaken for is a signal to quit. Whatever you do, put distance between yourself and negativity that's trying to turn someone else's doubt into your own.

Publishing content every day comes from the same place — creating the life worth living, and knowing what it actually takes to get there. Creating moves things forward.

## Conclusion

Building an online business is hard. Making daily content is hard. Producing a weekly podcast is hard. Building a life that supports doing what you love is hard — none of it was ever going to be easy. Self-doubt is real, but understanding where it actually comes from matters more than pretending it doesn't exist. There's no universal timeline that guarantees success in podcasting or business, but there is a guarantee that becoming good, confident, and skilled at anything takes time and patience — without both, it's not going to happen.
