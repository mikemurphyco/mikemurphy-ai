**Episode Summary:** A fundamentals episode on pixels, resolution, and aspect ratio — the building blocks for correctly sizing and preparing photos and videos for the web.

## What You'll Learn

- What pixels actually are, and why smaller/tighter pixels mean higher quality
- The difference between resolution/PPI for photos and resolution terms for video
- What 720p, 1080p, and 4K actually mean in pixel dimensions
- What aspect ratio is and the most common ratios used in online video

Understanding how photos and videos get prepared for online content makes it a lot easier to focus on the actual creating.

## Pixels

Digital photos and videos are made up of individual pixel units. When an image looks clean and sharp, the pixels are invisible; when it looks blurry or blocky, it's "pixelated" — the pixels themselves are visible. The "p" in 1080p and the "px" in 1920px × 1080px both refer to pixels. Smaller, more tightly packed pixels mean higher quality — and a larger file size. **Tip:** it's always better to size an image down than to try enlarging a small one.

## Resolution and PPI

"High-res" and "low-res" describe resolution — the density of pixels packed into an image. Those terms don't apply to video; video uses standard-def, high-def, or 4K instead.

- **High res** usually means print-ready, sized at real print dimensions — e.g., 5×7 inches at 300 PPI (pixels per inch).
- **Low res** is best for web and screens, typically 72–96 PPI. With Retina and 4K+ displays, some now recommend going as high as 150 PPI.
- **PPI** = pixels per inch. **DPI** = dots per inch (a printing term).

**Pro tip:** export at the lowest file size that still looks good, and nudge quality up only when it visibly degrades. Big, high-res images may look great, but they slow page load and frustrate visitors — trial and error settles the right balance over time. For web images in Adobe Lightroom, setting the "Long Edge" to 2048px at quality 80 is a solid starting point.

**Relation to podcasting:** episodes don't get exported at maximum quality either — the file size would balloon and take longer to download and more space on a phone, so the same balance applies to audio.

## Video: Aspect Ratio

Aspect ratio is simply the relationship between a video or photo's width and height — proportions, not size. The most common online video ratio is **16:9** (width:height) — the standard for horizontal YouTube video and most iPhone recordings, often called 1080p or HD.

**The one number worth memorizing:** 1920×1080 pixels at a 16:9 aspect ratio covers roughly 90% of video production needs. Video thumbnails follow the same 1920×1080 template at 72 DPI.

- **HD video (most common):** 1080p — 1920×1080px, 16:9
- **Standard HD (older):** 720p — 1280×720px, 16:9
- **Vertical video:** 1080×1920px, 9:16
- **Square video:** 1920×1920px, 1:1
